For a company pitch, position the three pages around **business value**, not just technical functionality:

### Page 1 — Live Water Network Overview

Give decision-makers an immediate view of network health.

Show current water pressure across locations/zones, sensor readings, normal operating range, and clear **Normal / Warning / Critical** indicators. The objective is to answer: **“Where is the problem right now?”**

### Page 2 — Intelligent Issue Detection

Turn raw pressure readings into actionable operational insights.

Automatically identify abnormal pressure drops/spikes, highlight the affected location, show when the issue started, its severity, and potential causes such as **leakage, pipe failure, abnormal demand, or sensor malfunction**.

The key message is: **“Don’t make operators search through data—the system tells them what needs attention.”**

### Page 3 — Recommended Action & Business Impact

Convert detection into a decision.

Show the recommended action, priority level, affected area, estimated water-loss risk, and potential operational impact. For example:

**Critical pressure drop detected → Possible leakage in Zone A → Immediate inspection recommended → Potential water loss prevented.**

### The pitch

> **“We are not building another monitoring dashboard. We are building a decision-support system that converts water-pressure data into early warnings and actionable recommendations—helping utilities identify problems faster, reduce water loss, minimize downtime, and prioritize field operations before small issues become expensive failures.”**

That last statement is what makes the project sound like a **business solution worth investing in**, rather than just a three-page UI.
